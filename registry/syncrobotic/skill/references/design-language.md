# SYNCROBOTIC design language

Everything here is a token in `syncrobotic-theme.css`; use the token, never the value.

## Colour: colour is for what needs someone
- Light is the default theme (`:root`): it reads outdoors, in daylight and glare. Dark (`class="dark"` on `<html>`) is for control rooms and night shifts. Let people choose Light, Match system or Dark (ThemeToggle). Both are complete; never design for one only.
- Surfaces: `--background` is the page only; `--card` for panels, cards and the fields on them; `--popover` for overlays; `--track` for tracks and quiet shapes (slider, progress, meter, ring, skeleton: 1.5:1 on the surfaces they sit on); `--muted` is the quiet fill (table headers, a segmented control's track, icon and code tiles, disabled fills) and the current page in navigation; `--accent` is hover (and a selected row, with its ink bar), one step darker than `--muted` so hover never looks like the current page; `--thumb` for the knob you drag (slider, a switch that is off), never `bg-white`; inside a tooltip, faint `primary-foreground` (it flips with the tooltip), never white at an alpha; `bg-scrim` behind a modal overlay and under words over a picture; `bg-letterbox` for the bars around a picture that does not fill its frame (never `bg-black`).
- Text: `--foreground`, `--muted-foreground`. Text never takes a series or status colour except the status word itself. Disabled controls keep their words readable, never opacity on text: a filled control (field, primary or secondary button) becomes `bg-muted text-muted-foreground`; an outlined one (outline and destructive buttons) keeps its outline (`ring-border`) with `text-disabled`, lighter than when enabled, never a heavier grey block; a text-only one (ghost and link buttons, tabs, segments, menu items) takes `text-disabled`, 3:1 on the page and lighter than secondary text. Say why it is disabled, beside it or in its label.
- Status is a shape and a word; colour only when it is abnormal. `warning` (needs attention), `danger` (needs someone now: stopped, failed), each with a `-subtle` tint; pair foreground on tint. A calm state (normal, idle, busy, patrolling, charging, offline) is grey: shape + neutral text. `success` green only for a confirmation that comes and goes (toast, alert), never for a state.
- Test and audit results (a test run, an evaluation, an audit): pass is grey with a check (neutral Badge, `CircleCheck`); fail is a danger outline and danger text (`CircleAlert`); not enough samples is grey with a dashed circle (outline Badge, `text-muted-foreground`, `CircleDashed`) and is never shown as a pass. Never a green pass: a pass is the expected result, a state, not a confirmation.
- Badge, InlineStatus or plain text. A state that changes (a robot's status, a job's state) is an `InlineStatus`: its shape and its word, no box, in a row or beside a page title. A fixed attribute or category (a tier, a data use, an environment, a version, Beta) is a neutral `Badge`; a count is a `CountBadge`. In a table column whose header already names the attribute, the values are plain text: a box on every row adds nothing. A header shows at most one status, then its badges after the title (`PageHeader`'s `status` and `badges`; never a `Badge` or a `MetaPill` for a state: neither has a status tone).
- Blue (`info`) only for a bounded process someone is waiting on: progress, a running stage, a mission in progress, a command being sent. The bar or cell of the running work is blue; the state word stays grey. Never for a state, never for links (links are underlined foreground; hover darkens the underline, not the colour).
- Levels that stay (battery, storage, signal) are a `Meter`, never a `Progress`. Battery: warning below 15 %, never danger, everywhere. A finished Progress is grey (`tone="neutral"`; it has no success tone), and an IconTile has no info or success tone: a calm state is `neutral`.
- A switch that is on is `--primary`, never green. A KPI's change takes colour only when it is worse; good news stays neutral.
- The environment badge (Production, Staging) is a neutral outline: a colour on every page all day stops meaning anything.
- Field edges (`--input`: fields, checkboxes, radios, a switch that is off) are 3:1 on the card, the page and a muted tile; dividers and card edges (`--border`) stay quiet.
- An overview puts what needs someone first: active alarms above the numbers and the tables.
- Solid red (`--stop`) is the emergency stop only. Destructive actions: danger outline and text.
- Focus ring is the foreground colour (`--ring`), so focus never looks like a status.
- Selected controls (segmented control, toggle) are inverted. Current page in navigation: muted fill + weight; hover: accent, a different, darker fill (never the same value as muted).
- Charts: graphite and one accent. `--chart-1` (the brand indigo) is the series the reader should look at; the others are graphite (`--chart-2`, `--chart-3`), `--chart-4` is the light grey for Other, `--chart-5` the baseline (an earlier period or a plan, dashed). Series are told apart by line style too, by order: solid, dashed (6 4), dotted (1.5 3.5), set by `ChartContainer` and drawn in the legend; bars and stacks keep a 2 px gap in the card colour. Three series and Other at most: fold the rest into Other, highlight one and grey the others, or split into small multiples; scatter plots and maps keep to the accent and one grey. Sequential (heatmaps): the accent's ramp mixed into `--track`. Never status colours for series. Every chart with axes (line, area, bar, heatmap) has a table view (`ChartTable`, a Chart / Table switch), for exact values and for anyone who cannot tell two hues apart; sparklines, rings (radial) and status strips write their numbers in words instead. Words or numbers drawn on a chart colour take its ink, `text-chart-N-foreground` (4.5:1 in every mode). Pick the form from the question first (/docs/foundations/data-visualisation).
- Annotations on images (boxes and points over a photo): line style first, colour second. Predicted solid 2 px (`--annotate-predicted`), ground truth dashed 2 px (`--annotate-truth`), the previous version dotted at `--annotate-fade` (`--annotate-previous`), selected 3 px with corner handles (`--annotate-selected`, the focus ink). Every stroke is drawn first in `--annotate-halo`, 2 px wider (3:1 against it), so it shows on light and dark photos. The class is a word in the label, never a colour; never status colours. Marks linked to something outside the picture (a hovered word, a row) are highlighted in the selected ink at 3 px, keeping their line style, and the rest fade to `--annotate-fade` (grounded-text); never a colour per link.
- High contrast: `data-contrast="more"` on `<html>` or a container, in light or dark, for bright sun and low vision: 7:1 text and status, 3:1 edges, nothing translucent. Set it from `matchMedia("(prefers-contrast: more)")` or a setting; meanings do not change.

## Type
- Sizes: xs 12 (hints, metadata, tags), sm 14 (buttons, rows, labels; a title inside a surface, medium), base 16 (body, inputs; bold: a page's section, `SectionHeader`, and a card's title, `CardTitle`), lg 18 (a page's title in an app shell's top bar, its h1; dialog titles; the emergency stop's label), xl 20 (sheet titles), 2xl 24 (the number that leads a card), 3xl 28 (a page's title in its content, `PageHeader`, outside an app shell), 4xl 36 and 5xl 48 (marketing heroes only). Nothing else. In an app shell the levels are 18 (the page), 16 (its sections and cards), 14 (inside a card): a page title is always larger than what is under it.
- Numbers are tabular (`tabular-nums`). A count is written in full with grouping (135,287) in tables, headers, detail pages and figures: anywhere it is read or compared. Abbreviate (135K, 1.2M; 14萬 in zh-TW) only where space is the constraint: a count on a tab, a chart's axis ticks, a compact chip; keep the full number one step away (the table view, a tooltip). Both through `formatCount(n, { compact, locale })` (`@syncrobotic/format-number`), never a hand-made "k"; any other number (a reading, a share, a price) through `formatNumber(n, { locale, ...options })` from the same file. Never `toLocaleString()`: without a locale it follows the runtime's language, so the server and the browser write different digits; the helpers write English when no locale is given.
- Weights: 400, 500, 700 only. Buttons medium (500); bold (700) for titles, figures and the stop.
- Titles inside a surface (a card's data header, a section inside a card or a sheet, a panel's head row) are 14 px medium (`text-sm font-medium`: `CardDataHeader` on a data card, `SectionHeader variant="surface"` for a section), never bold: bold is for the page title (18), a section's and a card's own title (16) and a sheet's title.
- WCAG large text is 24 px, or 18.66 px (14 pt) bold.

## Spacing, size and density
- 4 px base, steps 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32. Icon to label 8 (`gap-2`) everywhere: buttons, menus, tabs, nav, notes, alerts, alarms; the one exception is inside a 24 px Badge, 4.
- An indicator inside a rounded surface (an alarm's priority bar) is a pill with round ends in its own gutter, inset from the edge, never a straight strip against the edge: a round corner cutting a straight edge reads as a blade. A selected card takes a 2 px ink ring all round (as a graph node), never a one-sided border that the corner bends into a crescent; a flat row (table, list) keeps its 2 px start edge.
- Heights are tokens: `--h-sm` 36 (with a mouse only, at any density; 44 on a coarse pointer), `--h-md` 44 (minimum interactive), `--h-lg` 48, `--h-row` 56 (rows, bars), `--h-stop` 56 (stop, wake and slide to release only). Never hard-code a height: use the token so density works. A skeleton bone that stands in for a control takes the control's token (`h-(--h-md)`), never `h-10`.
- One part per repeated look, so it cannot drift: an empty place is `EmptyState` (`panel` on a card, `size="inline"` in a table, list or small panel, `variant="overlay"` over a canvas or picture); an icon on a tile is `IconTile`; a robot's ID or initials on a tile is a square `Avatar` (same corners as the tile of its size); a ⋯ menu is `DropdownMenuMoreTrigger`, a ghost icon button named "More actions for {name}" in a row and "More actions" in a toolbar ("View options" only for a menu of view settings), in a table inside `TableActionsCell`.
- A toggle group with one choice is a tool or a mode (what the next tap does); a view of the data, a sort order or a time window is a `SegmentedControl`. Narrowing a list by one attribute: a `SegmentedControl` when two to four choices cover the whole list and one is always on (All, Open, Resolved), each count after its word (`formatCount`, compact); a `ChipGroup` when the choice can be cleared, several can be on, or there are more than four.
- A toolbar is one height. When it holds a field (a search, a select), every control in it is the field's `--h-md`: outline Buttons at the default size, icon buttons `size="icon"`, never `sm` beside a field. `--h-sm` is for a row with no field in it (the `filters` chips and their + Filter), and is 44 on touch anyway.
- Density: `data-density="glove"` on `<html>` or a container (48 px controls, 64 px rows and stop) for gloved outdoor work; `data-density="compact"` (36 px controls, 40 px rows, mouse only) for a control-room console. The stop never shrinks.
- App shell: panels 8 px apart and from the edge, 16 px inside each. The sidebar's width and collapse belong to its edge: `SidebarRail` is a handle beside it (a window splitter), a short pill hidden until the pointer is over the sidebar. Drag it to resize (200 to 320 px; under 140 it eases down to the 72 px rail), click it or press Enter to collapse and expand, ← and → to resize; inset, it sits in the 8 px gap, over nothing. Never a collapse button in the top bar on wide screens; on phones the top bar's first button is the menu that opens the sheet. ⌘B / Ctrl+B anywhere. The top bar's title is the page's h1 at lg 18, larger than the sections and cards under it (16). Sheets float 8 px from the screen's edges too, every corner rounded.
- Action rail (a page that leads to one next step): the right column holds only that step, its title, one line, one `--h-lg` primary and a few small actions. On phones it is a floating bar fixed above the safe area, and the page is padded by its height. ← and Esc go back up a level, never while typing or with an overlay open. As a workspace it fills the screen from 1024 px with no page column: a leading column (a history, 18 rem, from 1280 px; below that a Sheet from a top-bar button) and a right panel (the fields, 22.5 rem, from 1024 px) with the rail pinned at its foot, 8 px apart like the app shell, each scrolling on its own under a `--h-row` head row so the hairlines line up. A top bar never wraps: items that do not fit fold into ⋯, last first.
- A top bar over plain page content is invisible at the top with its first and last controls on the content's edge; on scroll it gathers into a floating panel whose edges are the content column's.
- Pinning under the bar: every shell sets `--shell-top` (the bar's bottom edge in the scrolling area: top-bar-shell `--h-row` + 8, app-shell 0 because its page scrolls inside `<main>`, the phone shell its bar's measured height) and `--shell-surface` (the colour behind the page: background, or card in the inset app shell). `<TabsList sticky>` uses them; pin anything else the same way: `sticky top-[var(--shell-top,0)] z-10 bg-[var(--shell-surface,var(--color-background))]` with a bottom hairline (a filter bar). Under the bar (z-20), one pinned row per page. top-bar-shell sets the page's `scroll-padding-top` to the bar plus 8 px (plus a pinned TabsList while it is stuck), so a control that takes focus never stops under the bar (WCAG 2.4.11).

## Radius
- 2xs 4 (controls 24 px and smaller), xs 8, sm 12, md 16, lg 20, xl 24. Nested corners are concentric: outer = inner + padding when the inner part is within 8 px of the corner.
- `rounded-full` only for what is round by nature: avatars, dots and bullets, switch and radio, tracks and thumbs (slider, progress, meter, scrollbar), count badges, step circles, grips and grabbers, bars inside a rounded row (an alarm's priority bar, a selected row's ink bar).
- `rounded-[inherit]` is allowed, and only on a child that fills its parent edge to edge so it clips to the same corner: a scroll viewport, an avatar's picture, a link's hit area stretched over a card. It is the parent's step, not a new one; a product lint that enforces the scale should allow it by rule.

## Elevation
- One edge per surface: never an edge inside an edge. Inside a card, a sheet, a bar or a panel, a field is filled rather than outlined, a `Table` is `bare`, a `DescriptionList` is `bare` (or `tiles`), and a part is set apart by a hairline (`border-t`) and space, not a box of its own. The app shell's inset content panel is the page itself: cards and outlined fields sit on it as on any page.
- Surface: `bg-card ring-1 ring-border`, no shadow. Float (trays, save bars, toasts): `bg-float shadow-float ring-1 ring-edge backdrop-blur-xl`. Overlay (menus, dialogs, tooltips): `bg-popover shadow-overlay ring-1 ring-border`, with a scrim for modal ones.
- Over a viewport (a map, a point cloud, a 3D view, a camera): `ViewportOverlay` places the chrome 16 px from the frame's edges (controls bottom left, clear of the stop; what the view says about itself at the top), and each piece is a `ViewportPanel`, Float over a canvas or the scrim with light words over a picture, `rounded-sm` like the buttons. Never `bg-background` (the page) over a view, and never a card's ring with a shadow.

## Motion: movement only marks a change of state
- Tokens: `--motion-fast` 120 ms (press, hover, icon swap, exits), `--motion-base` 200 ms (state changes, appear), `--motion-sheet` 320 ms (panels and frames that change size). Every duration class has `ease-(--ease-standard)` beside it. Leaving uses the exit animations (`animate-disappear`, sheet-out, collapse), which carry `--ease-exit`.
- Six patterns (the Motion foundation lists where each is used): appear / disappear (`animate-appear`, `animate-disappear`), sheet in / out (`animate-sheet-in-*`, `animate-sheet-out-*`; scrims `animate-fade-in`), expand / collapse (`animate-expand`, `animate-collapse`), press (`press` utility, scale .97), swap (icon to icon, `--motion-fast`), move (a value or selection travelling, `--motion-base`).
- Between pages only the content moves (old: fade out, --motion-fast; new: fade in and 8 px rise, --motion-base). Anything inside a view transition cannot be clicked while it runs, so the root is never snapshotted (`:root { view-transition-name: none }`, `::view-transition { pointer-events: none }`) and the stop and the island are never inside one.
- Every enter has an exit (`usePresence` keeps leaving items mounted). Loops only for waiting: the Spinner, the skeleton's breath and the caret. Reduced motion: opacity only, loops stop.
- One exception, not for products: the docs site's home page is a showcase, and only there may sections enter on scroll (a 24 px rise and fade over `--motion-sheet`, items 60 ms apart, once, never reversing, nothing under reduced motion). A product screen never animates on scroll; its live data moves because the data changed.

## Touch and sound
- `@syncrobotic/feedback` holds one vibration pattern per safety moment (stop sent, confirmed, unconfirmed, release, new critical or high alarm) and the alarm tones. Use `feedback(name)`, never `navigator.vibrate` directly. Critical repeats until acknowledged; low is silent. Neither replaces the screen (no vibration on iOS; sound needs a first tap).

## Icons
- Lucide at 16, 20, 24 px, 2 px stroke, plus `@syncrobotic/robot-icons`. One icon per meaning: look the meaning up in the icon set (/icons) and use its icon; a new icon is a new entry there.

## Live data
- Live telemetry never freezes silently. Every component that shows a robot's live data goes stale by default when nothing new arrives, at a limit chosen for its data: camera feed 2 s, streaming chart 2 s, joint state and robot model 1 s, attitude 1 s, gauge 3 s, point cloud 3 s, diagnostics 5 s. Stale dims the data and says Stale (or Stalled, or No new data) with the last update's time. Pass the message's stamp (`updatedAt`, `receivedAt`) so a robot re-sending an old message still reads stale, and a still robot that keeps publishing reads live. `staleAfterMs={null}` is the one way to turn it off, only for data that is not live (a saved map, a recording, a URDF on show).

## Alarms
- Three priorities, each a shape, colour and word. An alarm stays until it clears and is acknowledged. Shelving is for a set time and never for critical; suppressed and out-of-service alarms are counted, not hidden silently. Over 10 in 10 minutes is a flood: name it, keep critical and high in view.

## Accessibility
- 44 × 44 targets on touch (48 in gloves); visible focus; never colour alone; icon-only buttons have aria-label; overlays trap focus and return it; new alarms are announced.
