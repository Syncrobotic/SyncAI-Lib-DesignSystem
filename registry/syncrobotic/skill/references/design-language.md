# SYNCROBOTIC design language

Everything here is a token in `syncrobotic-theme.css`; use the token, never the value.

## Colour: colour is for what needs someone
- Light is the default (`:root`): it reads in daylight and glare. Dark (`class="dark"` on `<html>`) is for control rooms and night shifts. Offer Light, Match system and Dark (ThemeToggle). Both are complete; never design for one only.
- Surfaces: `--background` is the page only; `--card` for panels, cards and their fields; `--popover` for overlays; `--track` for tracks and quiet shapes (slider, progress, meter, ring, skeleton: 1.5:1 on their surface); `--muted` is the quiet fill (table headers, a segmented control's track, icon and code tiles, disabled fills) and the current page in navigation; `--accent` is hover (and a selected row, with its ink bar), one step darker than `--muted` so hover never looks like the current page; `--thumb` for the knob you drag (slider, a switch that is off), never `bg-white`; inside a tooltip, faint `primary-foreground` (it flips with the tooltip), never white at an alpha; `bg-scrim` behind a modal overlay and under words over a picture; `bg-letterbox` around a picture that does not fill its frame (never `bg-black`).
- Text: `--foreground`, `--muted-foreground`. Text never takes a series or status colour, except the status word. Disabled controls keep their words readable, never opacity on text: a filled control (field, primary or secondary button) becomes `bg-muted text-muted-foreground`; an outlined one (outline and destructive buttons) keeps `ring-border` with `text-disabled`, never a heavier grey block; a text-only one (ghost and link buttons, tabs, segments, menu items) takes `text-disabled` (3:1 on the page, lighter than secondary text). Say why it is disabled, beside it or in its label.
- Status is a shape and a word; colour only when abnormal. `warning` (needs attention), `danger` (needs someone now: stopped, failed), each with a `-subtle` tint; pair foreground on tint. A calm state (normal, idle, busy, patrolling, charging, offline) is grey: shape + neutral text. `success` green only for a confirmation that comes and goes (toast, alert), never a state.
- Test, evaluation and audit results: pass is grey with a check (neutral Badge, `CircleCheck`); fail is danger outline and text (`CircleAlert`); not enough samples is grey with a dashed circle (outline Badge, `text-muted-foreground`, `CircleDashed`), never shown as a pass. Never a green pass: a pass is the expected state, not a confirmation.
- A state that changes (a robot's status, a job's state) is an `InlineStatus`: shape and word, no box, in a row or beside a page title. A fixed attribute (a tier, a data use, an environment, a version, Beta) is a neutral `Badge`; a count is a `CountBadge`. In a column whose header names the attribute, values are plain text. A header shows at most one status, then its badges (`PageHeader`'s `status` and `badges`); never a `Badge` or `MetaPill` for a state (neither has a status tone).
- Blue (`info`) only for a bounded process someone is waiting on: progress, a running stage, a mission in progress, a command being sent. The running work's bar or cell is blue; the state word stays grey. Never a state, never links (links are underlined foreground; hover darkens the underline, not the colour).
- Levels that stay (battery, storage, signal) are a `Meter`, never a `Progress`. Battery: warning below 15 %, never danger, everywhere. A finished Progress is grey (`tone="neutral"`; no success tone), and an IconTile has no info or success tone: a calm state is `neutral`.
- A switch that is on is `--primary`, never green. A KPI's change takes colour only when worse; good news stays neutral.
- The environment badge (Production, Staging) is a neutral outline: a colour on every page all day stops meaning anything.
- Field edges (`--input`: fields, checkboxes, radios, a switch that is off) are 3:1 on the card, the page and a muted tile; dividers and card edges (`--border`) stay quiet.
- An overview puts what needs someone first: active alarms above the numbers and tables.
- Solid red (`--stop`) is the emergency stop only. Destructive actions: danger outline and text.
- The focus ring is the foreground colour (`--ring`), so focus never looks like a status.
- Selected controls (segmented control, toggle) are inverted. Current page in navigation: muted fill + weight; hover: accent, a darker fill (never the same value as muted).
- Charts: graphite and one accent. `--chart-1` (brand indigo) is the series to look at; the others are graphite (`--chart-2`, `--chart-3`), `--chart-4` the light grey for Other, `--chart-5` the baseline (an earlier period or a plan, dashed). Series also differ by line style, in order: solid, dashed (6 4), dotted (1.5 3.5), set by `ChartContainer` and drawn in the legend; bars and stacks keep a 2 px card-coloured gap. Three series and Other at most: fold the rest into Other, highlight one and grey the others, or split into small multiples; scatter plots and maps keep to the accent and one grey. Sequential (heatmaps): the accent's ramp mixed into `--track`. Never status colours for series. Every chart with axes (line, area, bar, heatmap) has a table view (`ChartTable`, a Chart / Table switch); sparklines, rings (radial) and status strips write their numbers in words instead. Words or numbers on a chart colour take its ink, `text-chart-N-foreground` (4.5:1 in every mode). Pick the form from the question first (/docs/foundations/data-visualisation).
- Annotations on images: line style first, colour second. Predicted solid 2 px (`--annotate-predicted`), ground truth dashed 2 px (`--annotate-truth`), the previous version dotted at `--annotate-fade` (`--annotate-previous`), selected 3 px with corner handles (`--annotate-selected`, the focus ink). Every stroke is drawn first in `--annotate-halo`, 2 px wider (3:1 against it). The class is a word in the label, never a colour; never status colours. Marks linked to something outside the picture (a hovered word, a row) take the selected ink at 3 px, keeping their line style, and the rest fade to `--annotate-fade` (grounded-text); never a colour per link.
- High contrast: `data-contrast="more"` on `<html>` or a container, in light or dark, for bright sun and low vision: 7:1 text and status, 3:1 edges, nothing translucent. Set it from `matchMedia("(prefers-contrast: more)")` or a setting; meanings do not change.

## Type
- Sizes: xs 12 (hints, metadata, tags), sm 14 (buttons, rows, labels; a title inside a surface, medium), base 16 (body, inputs; bold for the page title in an app shell's top bar (its h1), the product's name beside it, a `SectionHeader` and a `CardTitle`), lg 18 (dialog titles; the stop's label), xl 20 (sheet titles), 2xl 24 (the number that leads a card), 3xl 28 (`PageHeader` title outside an app shell), 4xl 36 and 5xl 48 (marketing heroes only). Nothing else. In an app shell the levels are 16 bold (page title, product name, sections, cards) and 14 (inside a card): the page's title leads by place, in its own bar, never smaller than what is under it.
- Numbers are tabular (`tabular-nums`). A count is in full with grouping (135,287) wherever it is read or compared: tables, headers, detail pages, figures. Abbreviate (135K, 1.2M; 14萬 in zh-TW) only where space is the constraint (a tab's count, axis ticks, a compact chip), with the full number one step away (table view, tooltip). Counts through `formatCount(n, { compact, locale })` (`@syncrobotic/format-number`), never a hand-made "k"; any other number through `formatNumber(n, { locale, ...options })`. Never `toLocaleString()`: it follows the runtime's language, so server and browser write different digits; the helpers write English when no locale is given.
- Weights: 400, 500, 700 only. Buttons medium (500); bold (700) for titles, figures and the stop.
- Titles inside a surface (a card's data header, a section in a card or sheet, a panel's head row) are `text-sm font-medium` (`CardDataHeader`, `SectionHeader variant="surface"`), never bold: bold is for the page title and product name (16), a section's and a card's own title (16) and a sheet's title.
- WCAG large text is 24 px, or 18.66 px (14 pt) bold.

## Spacing, size and density
- 4 px base, steps 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32. Icon to label 8 (`gap-2`) everywhere; the one exception is inside a 24 px Badge, 4.
- An indicator inside a rounded surface (an alarm's priority bar) is a round-ended pill in its own gutter, inset from the edge, never a straight strip against it. A selected card takes a 2 px ink ring all round, never a one-sided border; a flat row (table, list) keeps its 2 px start edge.
- Heights are tokens: `--h-sm` 36 (mouse only; 44 on a coarse pointer), `--h-md` 44 (minimum interactive), `--h-lg` 48, `--h-row` 56 (rows, bars), `--h-stop` 56 (stop, wake and slide to release only). Never hard-code a height, so density works. A skeleton bone for a control takes its token (`h-(--h-md)`), never `h-10`.
- One part per repeated look: an empty place is `EmptyState` (`panel` on a card, `size="inline"` in a table, list or small panel, `variant="overlay"` over a canvas or picture); an icon on a tile is `IconTile`; a robot's ID or initials on a tile is a square `Avatar`; a ⋯ menu is `DropdownMenuMoreTrigger`, a ghost icon button named "More actions for {name}" in a row, "More actions" in a toolbar ("View options" only for view settings), in a table inside `TableActionsCell`.
- A single-choice toggle group is a tool or a mode (what the next tap does); a view, a sort order or a time window is a `SegmentedControl`. Narrowing a list by one attribute: a `SegmentedControl` when two to four choices cover the list and one is always on (All, Open, Resolved), each count after its word (`formatCount`, compact); a `ChipGroup` when the choice can be cleared, several can be on, or there are more than four.
- A toolbar is one height. With a field in it, every control is `--h-md`: outline Buttons at the default size, icon buttons `size="icon"`, never `sm`. `--h-sm` is for a row with no field (the `filters` chips and + Filter), and is 44 on touch anyway.
- Density: `data-density="glove"` on `<html>` or a container (48 px controls, 64 px rows and stop) for gloved outdoor work; `data-density="compact"` (36 px controls, 40 px rows, mouse only) for a control-room console. The stop never shrinks.
- App shell: panels 8 px apart and from the edge, 16 px inside. `SidebarRail` is a handle beside the sidebar (a window splitter), a short pill shown while the pointer is over the sidebar: drag to resize (200 to 320 px; under 140 it eases to the 68 px rail, where every link is a 44 px square 12 px from either edge on one centre line with the mark and avatar), click or Enter to collapse and expand, ← and → to resize; inset, it sits in the 8 px gap. Never a collapse button in the top bar on wide screens; on phones the top bar's first button opens the sheet. ⌘B / Ctrl+B anywhere. The top bar's title is the page's h1 at 16 bold, the size of the product's name across the gap. Sheets float 8 px from the screen's edges, every corner rounded.
- Action rail (a page that leads to one next step): the right column holds only that step: title, one line, one `--h-lg` primary and a few small actions. On phones it is a bar fixed above the safe area, and the page is padded by its height. ← and Esc go back up a level, never while typing or with an overlay open. As a workspace it fills the screen from 1024 px: a leading column (a history, 18 rem, from 1280 px; below that a Sheet from a top-bar button) and a right panel (the fields, 22.5 rem) with the rail pinned at its foot, 8 px apart, each scrolling on its own under a `--h-row` head row so the hairlines line up. A top bar never wraps: items that do not fit fold into ⋯, last first.
- A top bar over plain page content is invisible at the top, its first and last controls on the content's edge; on scroll it gathers into a floating panel whose edges are the content column's.
- Pinning under the bar: every shell sets `--shell-top` (the bar's bottom edge in the scrolling area: top-bar-shell `--h-row` + 8, app-shell 0 as its page scrolls inside `<main>`, the phone shell its bar's measured height) and `--shell-surface` (the colour behind the page: background, or card in the inset app shell). `<TabsList sticky>` uses them; pin anything else the same way: `sticky top-[var(--shell-top,0)] z-10 bg-[var(--shell-surface,var(--color-background))]` with a bottom hairline. Under the bar (z-20), one pinned row per page. top-bar-shell sets `scroll-padding-top` to the bar plus 8 px (plus a stuck TabsList), so focus never stops under the bar (WCAG 2.4.11).

## Radius
- 2xs 4 (controls 24 px and smaller), xs 8, sm 12, md 16, lg 20, xl 24. Nested corners are concentric: outer = inner + padding when the inner part is within 8 px of the corner.
- `rounded-full` only for what is round by nature: avatars, dots and bullets, switch and radio, tracks and thumbs (slider, progress, meter, scrollbar), count badges, step circles, grips and grabbers, bars inside a rounded row (an alarm's priority bar, a selected row's ink bar).
- `rounded-[inherit]` only on a child that fills its parent edge to edge (a scroll viewport, an avatar's picture, a link's hit area over a card). It is the parent's step, so a product lint enforcing the scale should allow it.

## Elevation
- One edge per surface, never an edge inside an edge. Inside a card, sheet, bar or panel a field is filled, not outlined, a `Table` and a `DescriptionList` are `bare` (or `tiles`), and a part is set apart by a hairline (`border-t`) and space, not a box. The inset app shell's content panel is the page: cards and outlined fields sit on it as on any page.
- Surface: `bg-card ring-1 ring-border`, no shadow. Float (trays, save bars, toasts): `bg-float shadow-float ring-1 ring-edge backdrop-blur-xl`. Overlay (menus, dialogs, tooltips): `bg-popover shadow-overlay ring-1 ring-border`, with a scrim when modal.
- Over a viewport (a map, a point cloud, a 3D view, a camera): `ViewportOverlay` places the chrome 16 px from the frame's edges (controls bottom left, clear of the stop; what the view says about itself at the top), each piece a `ViewportPanel` (Float over a canvas, the scrim with light words over a picture, `rounded-sm`). Never `bg-background` over a view, never a card's ring with a shadow.

## Motion: movement only marks a change of state
- Tokens: `--motion-fast` 120 ms (press, hover, icon swap, exits), `--motion-base` 200 ms (state changes, appear), `--motion-sheet` 320 ms (panels and frames that change size). Every duration class has `ease-(--ease-standard)` beside it. Leaving uses the exit animations (`animate-disappear`, sheet-out, collapse), which carry `--ease-exit`.
- Six patterns (the Motion foundation lists where each is used): appear / disappear (`animate-appear`, `animate-disappear`), sheet in / out (`animate-sheet-in-*`, `animate-sheet-out-*`; scrims `animate-fade-in`), expand / collapse (`animate-expand`, `animate-collapse`), press (`press`, scale .97), swap (icon to icon, `--motion-fast`), move (a value or selection travelling, `--motion-base`).
- Between pages only the content moves (old fades out at --motion-fast; new fades in with an 8 px rise at --motion-base). A view transition blocks clicks while it runs, so the root is never snapshotted (`:root { view-transition-name: none }`, `::view-transition { pointer-events: none }`) and the stop and the island are never inside one.
- Every enter has an exit (`usePresence` keeps leaving items mounted). Loops only for waiting: the Spinner, the skeleton's breath and the caret. Reduced motion: opacity only, loops stop.
- Only the docs site's home page may enter sections on scroll (a 24 px rise and fade over `--motion-sheet`, 60 ms apart, once, nothing under reduced motion). A product screen never animates on scroll; live data moves because the data changed.

## Touch and sound
- `@syncrobotic/feedback` holds one vibration pattern per safety moment (stop sent, confirmed, unconfirmed, release, new critical or high alarm) and the alarm tones. Use `feedback(name)`, never `navigator.vibrate`. Critical repeats until acknowledged; low is silent. Neither replaces the screen (no vibration on iOS; sound needs a first tap).

## Icons
- Lucide at 16, 20, 24 px, 2 px stroke, plus `@syncrobotic/robot-icons`. One icon per meaning: look the meaning up in the icon set (/icons); a new icon is a new entry there.

## Live data
- Live telemetry never freezes silently. Every component showing a robot's live data goes stale by default when nothing new arrives: camera feed 2 s, streaming chart 2 s, joint state and robot model 1 s, attitude 1 s, gauge 3 s, point cloud 3 s, diagnostics 5 s. Stale dims the data and says Stale (or Stalled, or No new data) with the last update's time. Pass the message's stamp (`updatedAt`, `receivedAt`) so an old message re-sent still reads stale and a still robot that keeps publishing reads live. `staleAfterMs={null}` turns it off, only for data that is not live (a saved map, a recording, a URDF on show).

## Alarms
- Three priorities, each a shape, colour and word. An alarm stays until it clears and is acknowledged. Shelving is for a set time and never for critical; suppressed and out-of-service alarms are counted, not hidden. Over 10 in 10 minutes is a flood: name it, keep critical and high in view.

## Accessibility
- 44 × 44 targets on touch (48 in gloves); visible focus; never colour alone; icon-only buttons have aria-label; overlays trap focus and return it; new alarms are announced.
